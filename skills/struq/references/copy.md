# Copy that sells

An award site that nobody understands is a demo. The studios at the bar write as hard as they design: every line is
short, specific and pulls the visitor one step toward the client. Write it in the client's market language, as a
native copywriter would, never as a translation.

## The first screen does three jobs

1. **A headline with a point of view.** It says what the client believes or what the visitor gets, in their world's
   words. Not a slogan about quality, not the trade word alone.
2. **A plain line under it** that says what the client makes or does, for whom and where. A visitor who reads only
   these two lines knows whether this is for them.
3. **The next step**, when the brief gives one: the real address, booking link, shop or mail, as a confident link,
   never a generic "Get in touch". With no door in the brief, ask for it at the one stop. A site that sells or takes
   enquiries ends on the order close; with no door given it carries the placeholder door from lib/site.ts, listed
   under Assumptions. Any other site with no door ends on the idea. Never an invented price, product, address or
   number.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Quality craftsmanship since day one. | Daylight, kept in glass. *Pendant lights designed and blown by hand in Bergen.* |
| Welcome to our bakery. | Bread that takes two days. *Sourdough baked overnight in Utrecht, sold from seven.* |
| Innovative solutions for your business. | Invoices that get paid. *Bookkeeping for freelancers, sent the day the work is done.* |

## Every scene after it

- **One job per scene,** said in one line before any detail: what the visitor learns here that moves them on.
- **Specific beats impressive.** "Blown at 1,100 degrees" only when the brief gives it. Never invent a number, a
  process or a quote to sound specific: the honesty rules hold, and a vague true line beats a sharp false one.
- **The client's lexicon.** Use the three to five working words of the trade from the plan in labels, captions and
  links, so the site sounds like the client, not like a studio.
- **Short.** Headlines under eight words. Body lines a breath long. Cut every adjective that could describe any
  business.
- **Verbs on links and buttons** that say what happens: "See the pendants", "Book a table for Friday", "Mail the
  studio". Never "Learn more", "Discover", "Click here".
- **The close lands the idea or opens the door,** in one line, never a summary of the page. On a site that sells or
  takes enquiries that line turns the idea to the offer, above the order close.

## Before you ship

Read the page top to bottom as a visitor who has never heard of the client. After the first screen, can you say what
they sell? After the last, do you know how to buy it, book it or reach them? If not, the copy is not done, however
good the motion is.
